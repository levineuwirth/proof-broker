## 1. Signing lifecycle (cohort v7)

v6 had `revise()` but no `sign()`. v7 adds the lifecycle and nothing else.

- **The checkpoint is preserved.** The disabled policy is written once as its checkpoint. `sign` writes a signed policy that
  differs from it only in `live_enabled`, the authorization, the authorized schedule, the matching money limit and
  `signed_from_checkpoint_sha256`. Every write is atomic: temp file, fsync, `os.replace`, then a directory fsync.
- **Scope is kept apart from the plan.** The planned design, 11 sites × 8 draws = 9,011,200 µUSD, is recorded as `planned_schedule`
  and `planned_micro_usd`. The signed schedule must lie within that plan. The money limit is exactly 102,400 µUSD per signed slot:
  1,126,400 µUSD for the eleven-slot first block. No $9.01 ceiling is encoded.
- **Signing refuses:**
  - a pricing capture older than the admission rule's maximum age, or dated in the future;
  - an approval time in the future;
  - a schedule outside the plan;
  - a money limit that does not match the schedule.
- **`sign` and `authorize` are resumable.** A crash at any persistence boundary resumes to the same state. The boundaries are the signed
  policy, the rehearsal ledger registration and the live activation. A torn first activation row is removed and re-activated.
  An activation row without its head is completed. A conflicting activation is refused. Recovery never creates a second ledger or
  resets consumption.
- **Later blocks require new authority** (`authorize`, same campaign, same request bytes):
  - a larger scope needs a different authorization with a later `approved_utc`;
  - the same scope keeps its authority or takes a newer one;
  - an older or same-time substitute is refused as a rollback;
  - consumed slots persist across revisions.

  A pricing revision under the same campaign is exercised the same way.

## 3. Frozen analysis

[`analysis_r6.py`](analysis_r6.py) is locked before any call. Its docstring is the specification. It reads `r6-analysis-input-1`,
which only the reviewed live auditor emits.

**Separate columns.** Returned, verified, consumed, local, whole and axioms are each a separate column. A stage counts only where
the previous stage is observed true, and an unobserved stage is `None`, never a failure. Audit and publication status are separate
from mathematical success.

**Dispositions and money:**
- slots are counted as pre-send releases (with attempt counts), exhausted slots, unknown or partial sends, and sent with a response;
- planned-but-not-collected slots are reported as neither evidence nor permission;
- costs are reservations, plus provider-reported usage (including cached input) priced at the frozen rates as a ceiling estimate.

**Witnesses.** Each witness is compared as its primitive integer vector over row names. A rescaled witness counts as the same witness, and
duplicates across draws are reported.

**Populations.** The map is 15 / 11 / 10 / 1 / 6 / 4. The exposure sensitivities are excluding l070 and excluding the `lift_cell`
family. The input also yields declaration-family grouping and prefix tables by draw. The deterministic arm is tabled beside the
learned arm with its reference kept separate. Any inconsistency in a population or denominator refuses the whole input.

**l170.** A false acceptance is any verifier or consumer acceptance of a Farkas contradiction for its satisfiable emitted rows. It is
an **integrity stop**. A correctly rejected proposal is not a false acceptance. Neither is proving the original obligation by another
route.

**Pauses.** These rules do not depend on proof success or witness novelty. Collection pauses on:
- an unknown or partial send;
- an audit rejection or publication failure;
- a transport-binding failure;
- an exhausted slot;
- committed money above the limit.

Controls: [R6-014-ANALYSIS-CONTROLS.json](reviews/2026-09-23/R6-014-ANALYSIS-CONTROLS.json), 12 cases: exact populations,
all-success, all-failure, mixed dispositions, missing and unknown responses, rescaled and duplicate witnesses, a partial collection,
l170 acceptance and rejection, costs, pause rules, malformed inputs, and the deterministic table.

## 4. Live auditor, on canned evidence

[`reviews/2026-09-23/cohort_v7_audit.py`](reviews/2026-09-23/cohort_v7_audit.py) is the approved R6-013 auditor. It carries v7's
identity and a `--mode live` whose changes are listed in the docstring:
- **Population.** Derived from each run's own evidence among five reviewed live kinds: proof, reconstruction refused, witness
  rejected, response invalid and provider error. Any other outcome fails closed.
- **Policy and ledger.** The signed policy must be its checkpoint plus the authority. The live ledger must serve exactly that
  authority.
- **Sender command.** Rebuilt in its live form: shared network, pinned public CA, operator credential outside the tree, no fixture.
- **Witnesses.** Checked by recomputation, never by equality with a canned certificate.
- **Operator scan.** The operator's disclosure scan (`reviews/2026-09-13/operator_disclosure_scan.py`) is bound:
  - the report is clean;
  - it carries the frozen commitment domain;
  - it was evaluated after every run finished;
  - it covers every run file and live-ledger file at its digest;
  - there is one receipt per credential-bearing run, bound to that run's seal and HTTP record.

under an isolated test policy directory and ledger:

## 5. What remains before a transmission

In order, each step separately approved:
1. review of this checkpoint;
2. the live-evaluation lock;
3. an operator approval naming the concrete scope, 11 sites × draw 1;
4. a fresh pricing capture admitted under the existing rules;
5. `sign` and activation;

a pre-send release and a refusal before reservation.
