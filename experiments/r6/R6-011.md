## Running the frozen stages on a site

The frozen stages reach an obligation through three task-specific pieces: the
anchor-line instrumentation (`run.instrument`), the manifest check
(`run.frozen_task`) and the registry (`task_spec.TASKS`, consulted by the event
log and by the supervisor). A census site differs in all three, so R6-011
supplies exactly those and reuses everything else unchanged:

- **Registry.** `site_task.get` verifies a site against the reviewed
  population (pinned decision digest, census and membership digests,
  exposure addendum binding, manifest schema and artifacts, decompressed
  challenge, expected policy) and only then registers it in the frozen
  registry *in process*. The registry file is unchanged; the two controls keep
  their entries; a conflicting registration is refused.
- **Supervisor.** The frozen supervisor runs in its own process and appends
  receipts through the frozen registry, so in-process registration does not
  reach it. `site_supervise.py` registers the verified primary sites and then
  runs the frozen `supervise.main` unchanged; `site_stage.stage` is
  `episode.stage` with one line changed — the supervisor it launches — and a
  control pins the difference to that line.
- **Instrumentation and helper.** The census freeze's byte-span instrumentation,
  renamed exactly as the frozen compile step renames the anchor form; the
  helper is the frozen `proposal_instrument.capture_source` applied to the site
  helper. A control recompiles a retained run's input byte for byte.
- **Setup.** The frozen `proposal_episode.setup` is task-independent apart from
  the expectation and the environment; it runs on the D1 control after the
  site's environment lock and compiled-environment inventory are shown equal to
  D1's, and the site's frozen expectation is substituted.

These two uses of the frozen registry are design points for review: in-process
registration of verified sites, and a supervisor shim that does the same in
the supervisor's process. The alternative, editing `task_spec.py`, would break
the fixture lock that every later harness verifies.

## Representability, fifteen sites

Each site ran the frozen preparation, the reification and SDK Farkas
compilation, with the captured context checked equal to the frozen one. The
problem was then classified against the frozen request policy
(`payload.request`: LIA, no compilation omissions, rows equal to the
independent projection, one `neg_goal`). Whenever the SDK produced rows, the
classifier also decided whether a rational Farkas certificate exists, using an
exact simplex over fractions, and checked the certificate exactly. That
certificate is an existence fact and a rehearsal fixture. It is not the
deterministic arm and it is never model-visible.

The reifier drops `hrec` (a `ZMod` equation) at the four `lift_cell` sites and a
`Finset` membership at l204. None of their certificates needs the dropped
proposition. Every drop is recorded per site as a hypothesis the model never
sees.

The denominator stays fifteen, whatever the class. Classification used only
the interface, never deterministic or learned success, and a site that cannot
be posed is reported as such, never as a model failure. The deterministic arm
is unaffected: the broker's own route runs on all fifteen, and its
representability differs from the learned arm's Farkas request.

## The decision the driver generalization needs

Generalizing the cohort driver means a new scientific contract. The frozen
contract (v1) pins the request schema by digest, and that schema enumerates
the two registered task ids, so no census site can be posed under it. Contract
v2 has to answer one question the classification makes concrete: **which
prepared problems may be posed to the learned arm?**

- **A — the frozen request policy.** 5 sites (l069, l070, l071, l078, l204).
  v2 differs from v1 only in the task enumeration.
- **B — pose the compiled rows when the compiler omitted hypotheses or the
  fragment is UF**, with the omissions recorded per site and shown in the
  audit (not to the model). Adds l096, l099, l170, l175, l178 → 10. l170 and
  l178 are posed knowing that no certificate exists over the rows. They are
  counted as interface failures for both arms, never as model failures.
- **C — B plus a locked extension of the independent projection to negated
  order comparisons**, cross-checked against the SDK rows. Adds l166 → 11.

The four SDK refusals (98, 101, 158, 180) stay unposable under every option
without changing the SDK.

**Recommendation: C.** The admission criterion is fixed at the interface,
before either arm runs. It does not select on solvability (it deliberately
includes the two sites that have no certificate), and it records exactly what
each posed problem leaves out. A keeps v1's semantics but poses one family of
four plus one site. That is thinner than the census review wanted, and it
drops sites the interface can in fact pose.
